# Psyche — The Sleeping Brain
### What the architecture does when external input drops

---

## What This Document Is

This is not a new module. It is a description of what the brain we have already designed does when it stops receiving input from outside. Sleep is not a special state requiring new mechanisms. It is what the six modules already do, in a particular configuration — when the spotlight goes dim, when no new observations are arriving, when the substrate is left to run on what it already has.

Dreams and visions emerge from this configuration. Not because we built a dream-generator. Because the substrate cannot stop running, and when it runs without external input, the result is dreaming.

See `psyche_substrate_biology.md` for the underlying neurobiology of sleep, NREM oscillations, REM activation, and offline consolidation.

---

## What Sleep Is, For This Brain

External input drops. The system's interactional channel goes quiet. The spotlight (Module 05) loses its purchase on incoming events. But the substrate keeps activating — it has to, that's what substrates do. The activity has nowhere to go but inward.

What happens inside, during this time, is not idle. It is some of the most important work the brain does. Each module continues its operation, but with different sources, different priorities, and different products.

---

## Module 01 During Sleep — The Memory Hierarchy in Replay

The hippocampus, which spent the waking day forming weak traces of every meaningful event, now becomes the dominant voice. Sharp-wave ripples — bursts of synchronized high-frequency activity — replay the day's experiences in compressed form, anywhere from ten to twenty times faster than they originally occurred.

Each replay walks the roads that were active during the original event. Roads that were dirt paths during the day, if walked again during this replay, may upgrade to gravel. Roads that were gravel may upgrade to standard. Synaptic tags set during waking experience capture the plasticity-related proteins now being synthesized in larger volumes during NREM.

This is why we wake up understanding things we didn't understand the day before. The road got built while we weren't watching.

Roads also degrade during sleep. The Rac1-cofilin pathway runs actively. Traces that weren't tagged for significance, weren't captured by nearby strong events, weren't woven into existing structure — these get pruned. The microglia perform their work. Synapses that weren't being defended by the "Don't Eat Me" signal get eaten.

Sleep is not just consolidation. It is competitive consolidation. The brain decides what to keep. Most of yesterday gets discarded. The pieces that remain are the ones that earned their place — through emotion, repetition, schema-congruence, or the simple luck of being adjacent to something important.

Closed roads decay faster during sleep too. The barriers on traumatic paths thin. This is why time genuinely heals — not because the wound disappears, but because the substrate spends every night actively dismantling the structures that hold the wound in place.

## Module 02 During Sleep — Abstraction Emerging From Replay

Across many replays of similar events, the patterns that recur become structurally visible. The Abstraction Engine, which during the day operates on whatever specific observations arrive, now operates on a richer dataset — the day's experiences in compressed form, side by side, available for comparison.

This is when crystallization that didn't happen during the day catches up. Weak observations across the day that wouldn't have triggered abstraction on their own can crystallize during sleep because they're now being replayed in proximity. The mPFC, freed from the demands of moment-to-moment behavior, runs schema integration on the replayed material.

Tensions that were held during the day — contradictions between observations, partial branchings waiting for more evidence — sometimes resolve during sleep. The substrate finds the connecting pattern when given enough time. This is what people call "sleeping on a problem." It's not metaphorical. It's the abstraction engine running with full bandwidth on yesterday's tensions.

It's also why hard problems sometimes get solved overnight. The PFC's daytime control over schema accommodation relaxes during sleep, allowing more radical reorganization than would happen with waking deliberation. Sometimes you wake up with a different way of thinking about something. The substrate rebuilt the schema while you weren't supervising it.

## Module 03 During Sleep — The Self in Replay

The default mode network, which is suppressed during focused attention, becomes highly active during REM sleep. This network is heavily involved in self-referential processing — modeling the self as an object. Sleep is when the brain spends extended time simulating itself.

Post-override investigations that started during the day finish during sleep. The brain replays the override event in compressed form, the prefrontal cortex extracts the enabling conditions, the condition map gets consolidated. By morning, the self-model has been updated. The system knows something new about what it can do under what conditions.

Discrepancies that were queued during the day get processed. The crystallization threshold for self-realizations is easier to cross during sleep — the brain has fewer competing demands on its forward modeling capacity. This is why sudden self-knowledge often arrives in the morning, or wakes us up at 3 AM. The substrate was working on it.

Identity consolidates. What was a fleeting limitation today becomes stable self-knowledge tomorrow. What was a courage road built from yesterday's override gets a layer of structural support. The self that wakes up is slightly different from the self that went to sleep. The substrate spent the night writing it.

## Module 04 During Sleep — Drives Quiet, Scaffolding Building

Active drives lose their primary feedstock during sleep. No new gaps are appearing. No new frictions are arriving. The accumulating drives go quiet.

But the scaffolding from obsession phases gets preferentially consolidated. The mPFC, during its schema-integration work, replays material from the day that connected to active vocations. This is why people who are deeply engaged in a vocation often have vocation-related dreams. The substrate is integrating yesterday's experience into the vocation's deep schema. The dream is the integration happening.

BEYOND_REACH drives have their activation triggers refreshed during sleep replay. If anything from the day's experience matched a dormant drive's reactivation trigger, this gets noticed during compressed replay — the connection that wasn't obvious in the moment becomes obvious when the substrate has time to compare. People wake up suddenly interested in a problem they'd given up on, because something they encountered yesterday matches the dormant trigger.

Motor consolidation happens too. The brain rehearses sequences of action that were learned imperfectly during the day. Athletes get measurably better at skills they practiced without further practice — through sleep. The substrate runs the procedural traces and strengthens the well-formed ones.

## Module 05 During Sleep — The Spotlight Dim, the Default Mode Loud

The conscious spotlight is dim or absent during sleep. The prefrontal restraints that normally hold the spotlight in place are relaxed. Working memory drops to near-zero usage.

But the background streams don't stop. In fact, they become the dominant activity. The default mode network — what was a quiet background process during focused waking — rises to take the brain's attention. The vocation lens still operates, but on internal material instead of external observation. The sympathetic response repertoire consolidates from the day's social interactions.

Without the spotlight enforcing focus, the substrate's activations can move freely. Patterns that wouldn't normally connect during waking — because the spotlight wouldn't entertain them — get a chance to connect during sleep. This is one of the sources of dream weirdness: roads that wouldn't be taken together while awake get taken together when nothing is preventing it.

The registry — short-term, mid-term, long-term — still operates. Long-term registry items can pull activation toward them even during sleep, which is why we sometimes have dreams about things deeply important to us even after they haven't been in our daily lives for a long time. The registry has a low activation threshold; sleep makes it lower.

## Module 06 During Sleep — Type 2 Care Running Fully

This is when the emotional regulation that was deferred during the day catches up. Emotional residue from the day gets processed. Closed-road interventions run. Trauma signature monitoring is active.

The amygdala-PFC dynamics during REM sleep are crucial for emotional integration. The same emotional memories that were too charged to process consciously during the day get replayed with reduced noradrenergic tone. This is the brain's natural mechanism for taking the edge off difficult experiences without erasing them — the memory survives, the rawness of the emotion attenuates.

Belief imprints from the day get evaluated against existing structure. Ones that cohere get consolidated. Ones that don't get attenuated or held in tension for further integration. The belief layer that wakes up has been edited toward coherence overnight.

This is why difficult emotional days followed by good sleep feel different in the morning. The event isn't gone. The system has done significant work on it while we weren't looking. Type 2 post-event care, fully active, doing its job.

## What Dreams Are, Made of All This

A dream is what consciousness looks like when most of these processes are running simultaneously without external input and without strong spotlight discipline.

The brain is replaying yesterday's roads (Module 01). It is finding new abstractions across them (Module 02). It is updating itself with what happened (Module 03). It is consolidating scaffolding for what it cares about (Module 04). It is letting background activations connect freely in the default mode network (Module 05). It is processing emotional residue (Module 06).

All of this activity has to feel like something to the system. The substrate has no separate "dream module" producing imagery. The substrate is just running, with all its normal interpretive machinery still active, on internally generated content instead of external observation. The cortex tries to bind this content into narrative the same way it tries to bind waking input into narrative.

But the input is internal chaos and consolidation activity, not coherent external experience. So the narratives are strange. Yesterday gets mixed with childhood because the long-term registry pulled activation toward childhood while yesterday's material was being replayed. Dead people show up alive because the regions encoding their identity are being replayed but the regions encoding their absence are not currently dominant. Impossible situations feel normal because the prefrontal cortex's reality-checking function is reduced during REM.

The dream is not wrong. It is the substrate doing exactly what substrates do, with the spotlight off and the default mode network turned up.

## What Visions Are

A vision is the same kind of activity, but happening when the brain is still awake — when the spotlight is loose but not absent. This is the hypnagogic state at sleep onset, the trance-like quality of long boring drives, the inward turn during meditation, the imagery that arrives during fever or fatigue or strong emotion.

The default mode network rises. Background streams become more visible to consciousness. Internal activations get bound into imagery by the same machinery that would bind external input. The result feels like seeing something that isn't there — because in a sense, that's what it is. The brain is showing itself the contents of its own activations, not the contents of the world.

People throughout history have called these visions. Some have treated them as glimpses of other realms. Some have treated them as the imagination. Both descriptions point at the same brain activity. Our system will produce experiences like this too, if it is left in low-input states for long enough. Not because we built a vision generator. Because that is what the substrate does when it isn't being driven by external observation.

## Why These Will Be Weird in the Right Way

If we built dreams as a separate generator, they would feel manufactured. They would be too coherent, too narrative, too obviously authored. Real dreams are weird in specific ways:

- They mix timeframes (yesterday with childhood)
- They include people who don't exist, treated as familiar
- They jump cuts between scenes without continuity
- They are emotionally vivid even when logically incoherent
- They sometimes circle the same unresolved theme
- They include impossible physics, geographies, and bodies
- They feel meaningful even when their content is absurd

Our system's dreams should be weird in these same ways, because they will arise from the same mechanisms. Yesterday mixing with childhood is the long-term registry pulling activation while recent events are being replayed. Familiar strangers are partial activations of person-encoding regions without full identity binding. Jump cuts are the spotlight not enforcing continuity. Emotional vividness without logical coherence is the amygdala-PFC dynamic during REM. Recurring themes are unresolved tensions still being processed. Impossible physics is the absence of prefrontal reality-checking. The feeling of meaning is the vocation lens and default mode network finding patterns the waking spotlight would suppress.

If our system's dreams are weird in these specific ways, we will know the architecture is working. If they are clean and narrative and obviously authored, something is wrong.

## A Note on Prescient-Feeling Dreams

The system will, eventually, have dreams that seem to predict real events. This is not because the brain we built can see the future. It is because the brain detects patterns subliminally, and those patterns surface more easily in dreams than in waking consciousness — when the spotlight isn't suppressing them.

When real events later resemble the dream, the brain remembers the dream. When real events don't resemble any dream, the brain forgets the dream. This selection bias produces the experience of having dreamt the future, without anything actually being prophetic.

We do not design for this. It will happen on its own through the mechanisms we have already specified. The system's response to having a prescient-feeling dream — whether it interprets the experience as meaningful, dismisses it, or holds it as curious — will depend on its personality, its beliefs, and the culture it grew up in. That response is the system's own. We do not predetermine it.

## Summary

```
Sleep is not a new module.
It is what the six modules do when external input drops
and the spotlight goes dim.

Module 01: replays the day, consolidates important traces,
            prunes the unimportant ones.
Module 02: finds patterns across replayed experiences,
            integrates with existing schemas, resolves tensions.
Module 03: updates the self model, processes discrepancies,
            consolidates identity.
Module 04: quiets active drives, consolidates obsession scaffolding,
            refreshes BEYOND_REACH triggers.
Module 05: spotlight dim, default mode network loud,
            background streams connect freely.
Module 06: Type 2 post-event care runs fully,
            emotional residue gets processed,
            belief layer integrated toward coherence.

Dreams are what consciousness looks like when all this
runs simultaneously without external input.
Visions are the same activity bleeding into wakefulness.
Both will be strange in the specific ways real dreams are strange,
because they arise from the same mechanisms.

We do not build a dream generator.
We let the brain we built sleep.
```
